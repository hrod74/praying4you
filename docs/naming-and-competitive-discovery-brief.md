## 1. Discovery frame

**Target user:** Believers who want to share prayer requests, pray for others in a community setting, and know they were prayed for, without the performance pressure or engagement mechanics of a conventional social app.

**Problem or job to be done:** People want real prayer support during hard, hopeful, or celebratory moments, and want to know someone actually prayed for them. People who want to pray for others want an easy, sincere way to find real requests and act on them.

**Proposed category:** Shared community prayer request and prayer support app (distinct from an individual prayer-tracking or devotional-reminder app).

**Current concept:** A shared prayer feed with named or anonymous requests, "I prayed for this" acknowledgment, categories, and (in discovery, not yet built) an eventual path to closing the loop with answered-prayer updates.

**Key assumptions:** That "shared, communal prayer" is a real and distinct enough need from "individual prayer tracking or reminders" to justify its own name and positioning. This assumption itself has not been tested through a full competitive teardown yet; the naming screen below already surfaces evidence relevant to it (see Section 2).

## Deep check on "Prayer Table" (2026-09-01)

Requested by the owner before treating "Prayer Table" as locked in. This goes beyond the Phase 3 pass above: a targeted trademark-database search, alternate domain variants, social handles, and a check of what "prayer table" already means culturally, since that context matters for both collision risk and for the differentiation story.

**Trademark database search.** Searching Justia Trademarks and uspto.report directly for "Prayer Table" surfaced no matching registration. The only tangentially related result was an unrelated mark, "PRAY U.S.A." No conflicting "Prayer Table" trademark was found in this pass. This is a positive signal, not a certainty; a full USPTO TESS search (structured, not general web search) or a trademark attorney's clearance search is still the right step before any legal filing or major brand investment, consistent with the workflow's own standard that this is an early screen, not legal clearance.

**Alternate domain variants.** Checked prayertable.org, getprayertable.com, theprayertable.com, and useprayertable.com. No live site or competing product surfaced at any of them. Combined with the earlier finding (prayertable.com held by a domain reseller, prayertable.app appearing unregistered), there is no evidence any domain variant is in a competitor's hands. A specific registrar lookup (e.g., checking availability and price directly at a registrar) is still the right next step to confirm and to pick the actual domain strategy.

**Social handles.** Still not independently confirmable through automated search. Search engines do not reliably index whether an unused handle exists or not, and Instagram in particular blocks automated fetching outright. No evidence of an existing "prayertable"-branded account turned up in search, which is a mildly positive signal, but this needs the owner to check directly (searching "prayertable" and close variants inside Instagram, TikTok, and X while logged in) before being treated as confirmed.

**What "prayer table" already means.** This is the most interesting finding of the deep check. "Prayer table" is a real, established devotional concept, not an invented phrase: Catholic and other Christian traditions use a "prayer table" as a small home or classroom devotional space (a table with a cross, candle, scripture, or other sacred objects, used for personal or family prayer). This shows up across religious education resources, home-devotional guides, and even physical furniture and decor for sale. This is not a competing software product or brand, so it is not a collision risk. It is, instead, real cultural and spiritual grounding for the name: "Prayer Table" would draw on a genuine, recognizable devotional practice rather than being an invented phrase, which supports authenticity. It also means the phrase itself is somewhat generic in the devotional-decor sense (physical furniture, blog content, home-education resources), so distinctiveness in trademark terms would rest on the software/app category specifically, where no collision was found, rather than on the phrase being wholly unique in general use.

**Owner's positioning idea, captured as stated.** The owner described the concept this way: the product's story becomes "leave and pick up prayers from the Prayer Table," people leave a prayer request at the table, and others pick one up to pray for it. This maps naturally onto the existing shared prayer-request feed and the "I prayed for this" interaction, and gives the app's core mechanic a warm, physical-world metaphor that is culturally grounded (see above) rather than abstract. This is a strong differentiation angle worth carrying into the eventual Competitive Landscape and Differentiation Brief's one-sentence differentiation statement (Section 5), once that section is completed.

**Updated recommendation:** "Prayer Table" holds up under the deeper check. No trademark hit, no domain squatting across five variants checked, no evidence of a competing social presence, and a genuinely positive cultural-resonance finding that strengthens rather than weakens the name. The remaining open items before the owner locks it in are: a formal TESS or attorney trademark search, a direct logged-in social-handle check, and a decision on domain strategy (.app if it is confirmed available, or a purchase negotiation for .com, or a different variant entirely).
